Your Day in the Cosmos.

Every day since June 16, 1995, NASA has published one Astronomy Picture of the Day. Pick your birthday, your anniversary, your wedding — any day that matters — and see what the universe looked like that day.

Good to know

Where do these images come from?

NASA's Astronomy Picture of the Day archive — one image or video of our universe, every day since June 16, 1995, each with a short explanation written by a professional astronomer. Images are shown for personal viewing with credit to their creators — NASA-created imagery is generally public domain, while many APOD images remain © their photographers as credited under each picture. Every entry links back to its original page on NASA.

Why can't I pick a date before June 16, 1995?

That's when the APOD archive began — there is no official picture of the day before it. (The universe was still there, just undocumented by NASA that day.)

Sometimes it's a video instead of a picture?

Yes — some days APOD features a video (a rocket launch, a timelapse of the sky). Those play right on this page.
