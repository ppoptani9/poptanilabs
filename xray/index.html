What is fund overlap?

Two funds "overlap" when they own the same stocks. A large-cap fund and a flexi-cap fund from different AMCs can easily share 40–60% of their portfolios — so holding both doesn't diversify you as much as the fund count suggests. Overlap is measured here as the sum of the smaller weight for every shared stock: if both funds hold 5% in HDFC Bank, that contributes 5% to overlap.

How to use this X-ray

  1. Search and add 2–4 funds in Step 1.
  2. Hit X-ray my funds — you get a pairwise overlap matrix, the full shared-stock list for any pair, and the combined top holdings across your selection.

Holdings come from the funds' latest monthly portfolio disclosures (equity holdings only — derivatives, debt and cash excluded). Disclosures lag by a few weeks; treat this as a point-in-time X-ray.

Frequently asked questions

How much overlap is "too much"?

There's no magic number, but as a rule of thumb: above ~50% overlap, two funds are largely doing the same job — one of them is probably redundant. Below ~25%, they're genuinely different bets.

Why do different funds own the same stocks?

Large, liquid index heavyweights (HDFC Bank, Reliance, ICICI Bank…) dominate most Indian equity portfolios. Active managers still cluster around them, especially in large-cap and flexi-cap categories.

Does overlap include foreign stocks and REITs?

Yes — foreign equity, REITs and InvITs are included; futures, options, debt, and cash positions are excluded.

Where does the holdings data come from?

Monthly portfolio disclosures as published via Groww's fund pages, cleaned and normalized. Updated periodically — the report shows the disclosure date.

Is my data stored anywhere?

No. Everything runs in your browser — no accounts, no tracking of your picks. See the privacy policy.
